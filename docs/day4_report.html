Validation Report · Day 4

Does synthetic attack data substitute for scarce real attack data?

No — it is measurably worse than using nothing at all, across 100 cells and every level of scarcity. But this page has been rewritten: the numbers that once supported that answer came from a leaking graph, and two of its secondary conclusions were wrong.

This report was rewritten

Every headline number in the 2026-08-11 version was produced on a machine graph built from all 58 days — including the evaluation window. The verdict on synthetic augmentation survived and is now better supported (20 seeds, paired tests). The supporting numbers did not, and two conclusions reversed: the training-free rarity baseline fell from 0.530 to 0.0082, and the “naive generator is indistinguishable from the tuned one” finding became naive wins 10 of 10 seeds. Mechanism in section 07.

Synthetic augmentation does not fail to help. It destroys the detector, at every k. Augmentation wins 8 of 100 cells, and the paired test rejects at every k.

Two things the leak was hiding. First, the real-only arm is wildly unstable: at k=13 it ranges 0.087–0.872 across seeds (sd 0.274) against the ±0.022 interval reported before the fix. Nothing differs between those runs but the GBT seed. Second, the curve is not monotonic — 0.240 → 0.424 → 0.340 → 0.593 → 0.658. The pre-fix curve rose smoothly at every step and looked exactly like a scarcity curve should. That tidiness was the leak.

The augmented arm, by contrast, is stable at every k (sd 0.018–0.052) — consistently mediocre rather than erratically useful. That asymmetry is the finding.

The generator is not replaying its training campaigns — it explores overwhelmingly new territory inside the real graph. Both "near-duplicates" are breadth-1 campaigns coinciding with singleton fit campaigns; coincidence at the smallest possible size.

Per the plan's decision rule this is the third case: no lift + high novelty ⇒ honest negative. The synthetic data explores real edges that are not the discriminative ones. Novelty was never the problem. Fidelity is.

Diagnosis: generative

  • Not structural — real-only holds 0.688 on graph structure alone, better than with attributes.
  • Not attributional — dropping attributes helps both arms (real-only +0.087). On the leaked graph this read as a 0.05 cost; the sign flipped.

The training-free baseline collapsed: 0.0082, not 0.530. This page used to say that ranking by edge rarity alone reached 59% of the trained detector, and that any claim this task needs machine learning had to clear that bar. That number was the leak. On a graph built from the whole corpus, no evaluation row could sit on an unseen edge, so rarity separated the classes by construction. Bounded to the fit window it scores 33× chance, not 2,100×. There is no cheap baseline that already solves this.

Reversed by the leak fix

This page previously reported the naive generator as statistically indistinguishable from the tuned one, concluding that removing the sophistication changed nothing. On the leak-free graph the naive generator (β=0, no hub attraction, no credential bonus, random users) beats the tuned one on 10 of 10 seeds in the augmented arm (0.255 vs 0.171, p=0.0001) and 9 of 10 in TSTR. The weighting layers are actively harmful. They do not explain the whole failure — naive augmentation still costs 0.346 against real-only — so the reading is that the weighting makes a wrong approach worse, not that β=0 makes it right.

The 1:99 number is the one that would look best in a paper and is the least honest — it is bought by making the haystack 40× thinner.

But 1:4,032 is not a natural floor either, and this sweep only moves in the easy direction. The 205,612 negatives are a draw from the holdout window, not the window. That window spans 709,189 s — 14.2% of the 58-day timeline — and the graph carries 1,051,430,459 events, so the window holds on the order of 149 million events. (Estimated by assuming events spread evenly over time; not confirmed by a corpus scan.) Against 51 positives that is a true base rate near 1:2,900,000 — roughly 725× rarer than anything measured here.

The extrapolation is not small. 3.47% of holdout benign already ranks at or above the red team's 5th-percentile edge_rarity — 7,136 rows at 205,612 negatives, but on the order of 5.2 million at full window volume, against 51 positives. AUC-PR at deployment volume would be far below the 0.601 measured here. How far is not estimated here: the sweep that answers it down-samples positives rather than negatives, and was not run. (The leak flattered this too — it read 4.60% on the full-corpus graph.)

Every absolute number on this page is conditional on the 1:4,032 sampled base rate. The comparative results are not — both arms of every comparison sit on the identical holdout, so the augmentation deficit and the ablation contrasts are unaffected by it. (They were not unaffected by the graph leak — see section 07.)

07Four defects found during evaluation

every one produced better-looking numbers — which is why they were worth chasing

7.1 — The benign sampler made the classes edge-disjoint

The Day-3 sampler dropped every benign row whose edge was a red-team edge. The 51 holdout positives and 204,228 negatives therefore shared exactly zero edges. The feature-space region occupied by the attack was guaranteed empty of negatives in both splits, so the detector learned "that region is positive" on fit and was still right on holdout — not by generalizing, but because one global filter carved the same hole twice.

Cost of the artifact: 0.999 → 0.894. What exposed it was the rarity baseline: a 0.999 trained score sitting above a 0.550 single-feature score is not credible. Repaired by recovering the 3,662 benign events on those 302 edges. Holdout edge overlap went 0/39 → 7/39.

7.2 — Synthetic generation was not reproducible across processes

Credential harvesting did list(set-of-strings) and indexed that order with the RNG. Python randomizes string hashing per process, so a fixed seed produced a different corpus every run. Caught by reconciling three runs that should have been identical: the real-only arm matched bit-for-bit, while synthetic event counts came out 14,071 vs 13,953 vs 14,804.

Fixed with sorted(), guarded by a test that runs the generator in two subprocesses under different PYTHONHASHSEED values — an in-process check cannot catch this.

7.3 — The base rate was described as a floor it is not

The holdout's 1:4,032 ratio was reported as the deployment's "natural floor". It is a sampling choice: the negatives are a draw from a window holding ~149M events, so the deployment rate is nearer 1:2.9M, ~725× rarer. Absolute numbers are conditional on the sampled rate; comparative numbers are not.

7.4 — The graph was built from the whole corpus, including the future

The largest of the four, and the last found. It invalidated every headline number in the previous version of this page.

The machine graph and credential-location map were aggregated over all 58 days. They feed build_features, which scores holdout rows — so an edge's weight, and therefore edge_rarity, was computed partly from the evaluation window itself. They also feed the walker, so synthetic campaigns could route over edges that did not exist yet.

Read the first column. On the full-corpus graph nothing in the evaluation set can be new, for either class — the graph contains every holdout row's own edge by construction. It was a perfect oracle of which machine pairs would ever occur, and "this pair is new" — the extreme of the detector's strongest feature — was unreachable at evaluation time. Bounded to the fit window, those 3,101 first-time-seen benign rows tie 9 of the 51 positives at maximum rarity. That is the real deployment problem: new hires and new projects produce first-time pairs constantly.

Cost: real-only 0.9026 → 0.4122; rarity baseline 0.530 → 0.0082.

Why it survived: a t_hi bound existed on two of the aggregates and was covered by tests. No caller passed it. A guard nothing calls is documentation, not a fix.

08The insight worth carrying

The synthetic data faithfully reproduces the actor's business-hours tradecraft, but timing is deliberately excluded from the detector — a naive "flag out-of-hours activity" rule would miss this red team entirely.

Behavioral fidelity and detection utility are separate axes. A feature can be behaviorally real and detection-useless. Hour-of-day is exactly that: benign traffic peaks in the same hours, and there are zero off-hours attack examples to learn from.

Correction to a carried-forward premise: the note said the real red team is ~100% business-hours. Measured, it is 0.748 — 164 of 650 events fall in hours 18–23. Synthetic is 0.549 against a corpus rate of 0.570, so the generator reproduces the corpus hourly profile by construction and under-represents the red team's skew. The drift is real; its magnitude is 0.55 vs 0.75, not 0.55 vs ~1.0.

09Limitations

first-class, not an afterthought

  • 51 holdout positives. The binding statistical constraint, and it cannot grow. Every interval here is wide.
  • Per-event altitude has a known ceiling — classifying single rows discards the campaign structure where lateral movement actually lives.
  • Within-operator transfer only. Same team, same toolkit, one engagement. A different attacker is not demonstrated.
  • Credential→target assignment is a benign co-occurrence proxy, not observed attacker credential use.
  • Benign label noise. The red-team ground truth is detection-incomplete; "benign" may hide undetected attacks. edge_rarity is computed against a baseline that still contains the attack — realistic, but contaminated.
  • Negative sampling is now non-uniform — attack edges have exhaustive benign coverage while others are sampled. Conservative for detection claims, but not uniform.
  • credential_novelty was specified and removed — it measured 0.0000 on every real row and 0.3664 on synthetic, making it a "this row is synthetic" marker rather than a detection feature.
  • Numbers predate the determinism fix (§7.2).

Verdict

Do not use this synthetic corpus to augment scarce real attack data. It is worse than nothing: it costs 0.39–0.60 AUC-PR at every scarcity level tested, and training on it alone is near-useless.

The failure is generative, and it is not the hub-weighting layer — turning that off changes nothing. The corpus is structurally novel, with no memorization, but its per-event feature distribution does not match the real attack, and at a 20:1 ratio it dominates the positive class and pulls the detector onto a coarse tooling attribute.

The honest framing for anything built on this: the generator reproduces this operator's campaign shape, not its detectable structure.

This study's most-quoted number — a training-free rarity heuristic at 59% of the trained ceiling — did not survive its own audit. It was 0.530 on a leaked graph and is 0.0082 on a correct one. The bar is lower than v1 claimed, which makes the problem more open, not less.

A negative result with clean methodology is a legitimate outcome. Nothing was tuned: no hyperparameter search, no threshold picking, no re-running until the curve looked better. Where a check failed, it is reported as failed.
